I am a mechatronics engineer with a deep interest in computer systems, automation, and the practical application of technology. My engineering foundation taught me to think across disciplines—mechanics, electronics, software, and control systems—and to see difficult problems as systems that can be understood and improved.

Today, I am especially interested in using that perspective to solve meaningful business problems. I enjoy turning ideas into useful systems that help people and organizations work more effectively, make better decisions, and create lasting value.

## Learning from first principles

I am a curious learner who enjoys reading widely, questioning assumptions, and connecting ideas across fields. For me, acquiring knowledge is only the beginning; the more important challenge is finding ways to apply it and make it valuable to others.

My preferred approach is to begin with first principles: understand the fundamentals, examine the evidence, and build upward from what can be clearly explained. Philosophy helps me ask better questions, mathematics brings precision to my reasoning, and the scientific method provides a disciplined way to test ideas against reality.

## Entrepreneurship and leadership

I aspire to build ventures that address worthwhile problems, particularly where technology, automation, and business strategy meet. I am drawn to the work of discovering a real need, designing a thoughtful solution, and creating an organization capable of delivering it well.

I see leadership as a practice rather than a title. It requires sound judgment, clear communication, the ability to help others succeed, and a willingness to take responsibility for outcomes. This blog is one place where I develop that practice: by making my thinking explicit, sharing what I learn, and remaining open to perspectives that challenge my own.

## Beyond work

I enjoy playing chess because it rewards patience, strategic thinking, and careful decisions under uncertainty. I also follow football, basketball, and tennis.

Literature is another source of reflection. Rudyard Kipling is my favorite author, and his poem *If—* continues to resonate with me through its meditation on character, composure, and perseverance. My favorite book is John Bunyan's *The Pilgrim's Progress*, whose portrayal of conviction and the difficult journey toward a worthy destination has stayed with me. I also return to writers such as Ralph Waldo Emerson and Napoleon Hill for their ideas about self-reliance, purpose, and initiative.
